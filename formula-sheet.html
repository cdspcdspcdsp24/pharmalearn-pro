Absorption

Absolute bioavailability

F = (AUC oral / AUC IV) x (Dose IV / Dose oral)

Compare oral product with IV reference.

  • F: fraction reaching systemic circulation
  • AUC oral: exposure after oral dose
  • AUC IV: exposure after IV dose

Solved example: Oral AUC 40 mg.h/L after 200 mg; IV AUC 100 mg.h/L after 100 mg. F=(40/100)x(100/200)=0.20=20%.

Absorption

Relative bioavailability

Frel = (AUC test / AUC standard) x (Dose standard / Dose test)

Compare two oral dosage forms.

  • Frel: test product availability compared with standard
  • AUC: total exposure

Solved example: Test AUC 90, standard AUC 100, same dose. Frel=90/100=90%.

Absorption

Fick law

Rate = (D x A x K x Delta C) / h

Explain passive diffusion across biological membranes.

  • D: diffusion coefficient
  • A: surface area
  • K: partition coefficient
  • Delta C: concentration difference
  • h: membrane thickness

Solved example: If surface area doubles, rate doubles when other factors are constant.

Distribution

Volume of distribution

Vd = Amount of drug in body / Plasma concentration

Estimate tissue distribution and loading dose.

  • Vd: apparent volume
  • Amount: drug in body
  • Plasma concentration: measured blood level

Solved example: Dose 500 mg gives Cp 10 mg/L. Vd=500/10=50 L.

Distribution

Loading dose

LD = Target Cp x Vd / F

Reach therapeutic level quickly.

  • LD: loading dose
  • Target Cp: desired plasma concentration
  • F: bioavailability

Solved example: Target 5 mg/L, Vd 40 L, F 0.5. LD=5x40/0.5=400 mg.

Metabolism

Michaelis-Menten rate

Rate = (Vmax x C) / (Km + C)

Describe saturable metabolism.

  • Vmax: maximum metabolic rate
  • C: drug concentration
  • Km: concentration at half Vmax

Solved example: Vmax 100 mg/h, Km 10 mg/L, C 10 mg/L. Rate=100x10/(10+10)=50 mg/h.

Metabolism

Intrinsic clearance

CLint = Vmax / Km

Compare metabolic ability of enzyme systems.

  • CLint: enzyme capacity when concentration is low
  • Vmax: maximum rate
  • Km: affinity constant

Solved example: Vmax 80, Km 20. CLint=4 L/h.

Metabolism

Hepatic extraction ratio

E = (Cin - Cout) / Cin

Classify high and low extraction drugs.

  • Cin: drug concentration entering liver
  • Cout: leaving liver

Solved example: Cin 100, Cout 30. E=70/100=0.7 high extraction.

Excretion

Renal clearance

CLr = Ae / AUC

Measure kidney removal of unchanged drug.

  • CLr: renal clearance
  • Ae: amount excreted unchanged in urine
  • AUC: plasma exposure

Solved example: Ae 300 mg, AUC 50 mg.h/L. CLr=6 L/h.

Excretion

Fraction excreted unchanged

fe = Ae / Dose

Know renal contribution to elimination.

  • fe: fraction excreted unchanged
  • Ae: unchanged amount in urine

Solved example: Ae 40 mg from 100 mg dose. fe=0.4 or 40%.

Excretion

Half-life

t1/2 = 0.693 x Vd / CL

Predict accumulation and washout.

  • t1/2: half-life
  • Vd: volume of distribution
  • CL: total clearance

Solved example: Vd 50 L, CL 5 L/h. t1/2=0.693x50/5=6.93 h.

Intravenous Bolus Injection

Concentration-time

C = C0 x e^(-k t)

Predict plasma level after IV bolus.

  • C: concentration at time t
  • C0: initial concentration
  • k: elimination rate constant
  • t: time

Solved example: C0 20 mg/L, k 0.2 h-1, t 5 h. C=20xe^-1=7.36 mg/L.

Intravenous Bolus Injection

Half-life

t1/2 = 0.693 / k

Convert between k and half-life.

  • t1/2: half-life
  • k: elimination rate constant

Solved example: k 0.231 h-1. t1/2=0.693/0.231=3 h.

Intravenous Bolus Injection

Vd after IV bolus

Vd = Dose / C0

Estimate distribution volume.

  • Dose: IV dose
  • C0: initial concentration

Solved example: Dose 1000 mg, C0 20 mg/L. Vd=50 L.

Intravenous Bolus Injection

Clearance

CL = k x Vd

Find clearance from bolus data.

  • CL: total clearance
  • k: elimination rate constant
  • Vd: volume of distribution

Solved example: k 0.1 h-1, Vd 40 L. CL=4 L/h.

Intravenous Infusion and Extravascular Administration

Steady-state concentration

Css = R0 / CL

Choose infusion rate for target level.

  • Css: steady-state concentration
  • R0: infusion rate
  • CL: clearance

Solved example: R0 50 mg/h, CL 5 L/h. Css=10 mg/L.

Intravenous Infusion and Extravascular Administration

Infusion rise

C = Css x (1 - e^(-k t))

Predict level before steady state.

  • C: concentration during infusion
  • Css: steady-state concentration
  • k: elimination rate constant

Solved example: Css 20, k 0.2, t 5. C=20(1-e^-1)=12.64 mg/L.

Intravenous Infusion and Extravascular Administration

Loading dose

LD = Css x Vd

Reach target quickly.

  • LD: loading dose
  • Css: target concentration
  • Vd: volume of distribution

Solved example: Css 10 mg/L, Vd 30 L. LD=300 mg.

Intravenous Infusion and Extravascular Administration

Extravascular concentration

C = (F x Ka x Dose)/(Vd x (Ka-k)) x (e^(-k t)-e^(-Ka t))

Describe oral/IM concentration-time profile.

  • F: bioavailability
  • Ka: absorption constant
  • k: elimination constant

Solved example: Used to explain rising and falling curve; numerical substitution follows calculator steps.

Multi-Compartment Models

Two-compartment concentration

C = A x e^(-alpha t) + B x e^(-beta t)

Fit biexponential plasma curves.

  • A, B: intercepts for distribution and elimination components
  • alpha: distribution hybrid rate constant
  • beta: elimination hybrid rate constant

Solved example: A=20, alpha=1, B=5, beta=0.1, t=2. C=20e^-2 + 5e^-0.2 = 2.71 + 4.09 = 6.80 mg/L.

Multi-Compartment Models

Distribution half-life

t1/2 alpha = 0.693 / alpha

Find time for distribution phase to reduce by half.

  • alpha: distribution rate constant

Solved example: alpha 0.693 h-1 gives t1/2 alpha 1 h.

Multi-Compartment Models

Elimination half-life

t1/2 beta = 0.693 / beta

Find terminal half-life.

  • beta: terminal elimination rate constant

Solved example: beta 0.0693 h-1 gives t1/2 beta 10 h.
