Multi-Compartment Models

Models where drug moves between central and peripheral compartments with different rates.

Advanced pharmacokinetics and compartment modelingEnglish + TanglishExam focused

First Understand

What is topic? Models where drug moves between central and peripheral compartments with different rates.

Why important? Many drugs do not distribute instantly; multi-compartment models explain rapid distribution, slow elimination, tissue reservoirs, and complex curves.

Clinical applications: Designing dosing for anesthetics, aminoglycosides, anticancer drugs, digoxin, and drugs with deep tissue distribution.

Every Heading and Subheading

Central Compartment

Simple English: Blood and highly perfused organs where drug appears quickly.

Tanglish: Blood, heart, liver, kidney maari fast reach aagura area central compartment.

Real-life example: IV drug first central compartment-la appear aagum.

Memory trick: Central = circulation core.

Peripheral Compartment

Simple English: Less perfused tissues where drug distributes more slowly.

Tanglish: Muscle/fat maari slow reach tissue peripheral.

Real-life example: Diazepam can enter fat and prolong effect.

Memory trick: Peripheral = slower storage.

Two-Compartment Curve

Simple English: Plasma concentration declines in two phases: alpha distribution phase and beta elimination phase.

Tanglish: First steep fall distribution; later slow fall elimination.

Real-life example: Graph-la two slopes irukkum.

Memory trick: Alpha arrives fast; beta leaves slow.

Micro and Macro Constants

Simple English: Microconstants describe transfer between compartments; macroconstants alpha and beta describe observed curve slopes.

Tanglish: k12, k21, k10 movement constants; alpha beta graph slopes.

Real-life example: Used in PK modeling software.

Memory trick: Micro moves; macro measures.

Method of Residuals

Simple English: Also called feathering; separates biexponential curve into distribution and elimination components.

Tanglish: Curve-ai two straight lines-aa split panni constants calculate pannuvom.

Real-life example: Used in two-compartment analysis.

Memory trick: Feathering separates phases.

Formula Explanations and Solved Problems

Two-compartment concentration

C = A x e^(-alpha t) + B x e^(-beta t)

Why used: Fit biexponential plasma curves.

  • A, B: intercepts for distribution and elimination components
  • alpha: distribution hybrid rate constant
  • beta: elimination hybrid rate constant

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: A=20, alpha=1, B=5, beta=0.1, t=2. C=20e^-2 + 5e^-0.2 = 2.71 + 4.09 = 6.80 mg/L.

Distribution half-life

t1/2 alpha = 0.693 / alpha

Why used: Find time for distribution phase to reduce by half.

  • alpha: distribution rate constant

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: alpha 0.693 h-1 gives t1/2 alpha 1 h.

Elimination half-life

t1/2 beta = 0.693 / beta

Why used: Find terminal half-life.

  • beta: terminal elimination rate constant

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: beta 0.0693 h-1 gives t1/2 beta 10 h.
