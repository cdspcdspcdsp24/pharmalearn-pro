Intravenous Infusion and Extravascular Administration

Drug input by constant IV infusion or by absorption from extravascular routes such as oral or IM.

Pharmacokinetics, input-rate modelsEnglish + TanglishExam focused

First Understand

What is topic? Drug input by constant IV infusion or by absorption from extravascular routes such as oral or IM.

Why important? Infusion controls plasma levels safely; extravascular equations explain absorption plus elimination together.

Clinical applications: Designing drips, maintaining steady concentration, loading dose before infusion, oral dosing interpretation, and switching from IV to oral therapy.

Every Heading and Subheading

Constant Rate Infusion

Simple English: Drug enters at a constant rate while elimination removes drug. Concentration rises toward steady state.

Tanglish: Infusion-la input constant. Body remove pannum. Balance aana steady state.

Real-life example: Vancomycin infusion aims for target exposure.

Memory trick: Infusion climbs to plateau.

Steady State Css

Simple English: At steady state, rate of input equals rate of output.

Tanglish: Input rate = output rate aana Css.

Real-life example: Dose rate is adjusted using clearance.

Memory trick: Steady state is balance state.

Loading Dose with Infusion

Simple English: Loading dose can rapidly reach target concentration while infusion maintains it.

Tanglish: Immediate target venumna loading dose kuduthu, infusion maintain pannuvom.

Real-life example: Lidocaine infusion often uses loading dose.

Memory trick: Load fast, drip steady.

Post-Infusion Decline

Simple English: After stopping infusion, concentration falls by first-order elimination.

Tanglish: Infusion stop pannina apram bolus maari decline aagum.

Real-life example: Stopping toxic infusion reduces level according to half-life.

Memory trick: Stop drip, elimination takes over.

Extravascular Absorption

Simple English: After oral/IM/SC dose, concentration rises by absorption and falls by elimination.

Tanglish: Outside vein route-la drug first absorb aagi apram eliminate aagum.

Real-life example: Oral antibiotic concentration-time curve has Cmax and Tmax.

Memory trick: Absorb up, eliminate down.

Formula Explanations and Solved Problems

Steady-state concentration

Css = R0 / CL

Why used: Choose infusion rate for target level.

  • Css: steady-state concentration
  • R0: infusion rate
  • CL: clearance

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: R0 50 mg/h, CL 5 L/h. Css=10 mg/L.

Infusion rise

C = Css x (1 - e^(-k t))

Why used: Predict level before steady state.

  • C: concentration during infusion
  • Css: steady-state concentration
  • k: elimination rate constant

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Css 20, k 0.2, t 5. C=20(1-e^-1)=12.64 mg/L.

Loading dose

LD = Css x Vd

Why used: Reach target quickly.

  • LD: loading dose
  • Css: target concentration
  • Vd: volume of distribution

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Css 10 mg/L, Vd 30 L. LD=300 mg.

Extravascular concentration

C = (F x Ka x Dose)/(Vd x (Ka-k)) x (e^(-k t)-e^(-Ka t))

Why used: Describe oral/IM concentration-time profile.

  • F: bioavailability
  • Ka: absorption constant
  • k: elimination constant

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Used to explain rising and falling curve; numerical substitution follows calculator steps.

Graph Explanations

Infusion curve

X-axis time; Y-axis concentration. It rises asymptotically to Css; about 50 percent at one half-life, 75 percent at two, 87.5 percent at three, and over 95 percent at four to five half-lives.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks time to steady state and effect of changing infusion rate.

Extravascular curve

X-axis time; Y-axis concentration. Absorption phase rises to Cmax/Tmax; elimination phase falls.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks to label Ka, k, Cmax, Tmax and AUC.
