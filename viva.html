Question, answer, and short explanation for quick oral exam preparation.

Absorption: What is Absorption?

Answer: Entry of drug from administration site into systemic circulation.

Short explanation: Drug blood-ku enter aagura step. Clinical/exam use: Used to predict onset and intensity.

Absorption: What is Bioavailability (F)?

Answer: Fraction of administered dose reaching systemic circulation unchanged.

Short explanation: Dose-la blood-ku useful-aa reach aagura portion. Clinical/exam use: High F usually means lower oral dose needed.

Absorption: What is Cmax?

Answer: Maximum plasma concentration after a dose.

Short explanation: Blood-la highest drug level. Clinical/exam use: Related to peak effect and toxicity.

Absorption: What is Tmax?

Answer: Time required to reach Cmax.

Short explanation: Peak level vara edukkura time. Clinical/exam use: Related to onset speed.

Absorption: What is AUC?

Answer: Area under plasma concentration-time curve.

Short explanation: Total exposure. Clinical/exam use: Used to compare products and calculate bioavailability.

Distribution: What is Distribution?

Answer: Movement of drug from systemic blood to tissues and back.

Short explanation: Blood-lendhu tissues-ku spread aaguradhu. Clinical/exam use: Explains target-site concentration.

Distribution: What is Vd?

Answer: Apparent volume needed to contain total drug at plasma concentration.

Short explanation: Drug body-la evlo spread aachu nu sollura number. Clinical/exam use: Used to calculate loading dose.

Distribution: What is Free drug?

Answer: Unbound drug in plasma.

Short explanation: Protein-ku attach aagala drug. Clinical/exam use: Pharmacologically active and clearable fraction.

Distribution: What is Protein binding?

Answer: Reversible binding of drug to plasma proteins.

Short explanation: Drug albumin-kitta temporary hold aaguradhu. Clinical/exam use: Affects interactions and toxicity.

Metabolism: What is Metabolism?

Answer: Chemical alteration of drug by enzymes.

Short explanation: Drug-ai body chemical-aa maathuradhu. Clinical/exam use: Controls activity and clearance.

Metabolism: What is Prodrug?

Answer: Inactive or less active compound converted to active drug.

Short explanation: Body activate panna vendiya drug. Clinical/exam use: Used to improve absorption or targeting.

Metabolism: What is CYP450?

Answer: Major oxidative enzyme family in liver and intestine.

Short explanation: Liver-la drug break/change panna important enzymes. Clinical/exam use: Major source of interactions.

Metabolism: What is Induction?

Answer: Increase in enzyme activity or amount.

Short explanation: Enzyme increase. Clinical/exam use: May cause treatment failure.

Metabolism: What is Inhibition?

Answer: Decrease in enzyme activity.

Short explanation: Enzyme block. Clinical/exam use: May cause toxicity.

Excretion: What is Excretion?

Answer: Irreversible loss of drug from body.

Short explanation: Drug veliya poguradhu. Clinical/exam use: Final ADME step.

Short explanation: Level half aaga edukkura time. Clinical/exam use: Used for dosing interval and steady state.

Excretion: What is Enterohepatic recycling?

Multi-Compartment Models: What is Compartment?

Answer: Kinetic space where drug behaves similarly, not necessarily an anatomical organ.

Short explanation: Mathematical box. Clinical/exam use: Simplifies dosing calculations.

Multi-Compartment Models: What is Alpha phase?

Answer: Rapid distribution phase.

Short explanation: Fast distribution drop. Clinical/exam use: Initial slope.

Multi-Compartment Models: What is Beta phase?

Answer: Slower elimination phase.

Short explanation: Slow elimination drop. Clinical/exam use: Terminal slope.

Multi-Compartment Models: What is Hybrid constants?

Answer: Alpha and beta constants seen from plasma curve.

Short explanation: Observed slopes. Clinical/exam use: Used to calculate half-lives.
