Distribution

Reversible transfer of drug between blood and body tissues after absorption or IV entry.

Pharmacokinetics, ADMEEnglish + TanglishExam focused

First Understand

What is topic? Reversible transfer of drug between blood and body tissues after absorption or IV entry.

Why important? Distribution decides where drug acts, where toxicity occurs, loading dose, tissue storage, and duration.

Clinical applications: Dose adjustment in obesity, edema, pregnancy, hypoalbuminemia, burns, renal failure, and CNS infections where barriers change.

Every Heading and Subheading

Apparent Volume of Distribution

Simple English: Vd relates amount of drug in body to plasma concentration; it is apparent, not a real anatomical volume.

Tanglish: Body-la irukkura total drug amount-ai plasma concentration oda compare pannum value dhaan Vd.

Real-life example: Digoxin has high Vd because it enters tissues strongly.

Memory trick: Vd: Vast distribution means high Vd.

Plasma Protein Binding

Simple English: Albumin binds acidic drugs; alpha-1 acid glycoprotein binds basic drugs. Only free drug usually distributes, acts, and clears.

Tanglish: Protein bind aana drug temporarily inactive maari irukkum; free drug dhaan action pannum.

Real-life example: Warfarin is highly albumin bound; displacement can increase bleeding risk.

Memory trick: Free drug is the active traveler.

Tissue Binding

Simple English: Some drugs bind to fat, bone, muscle, or melanin and create reservoirs.

Tanglish: Drug tissue-la store aagalam; appo long duration varum.

Real-life example: Tetracycline binds calcium in teeth and bone.

Memory trick: Storage tissue slows goodbye.

Blood-Brain and Placental Barriers

Simple English: Tight junctions and transporters restrict drug entry into brain; placenta allows many lipid-soluble drugs to cross.

Tanglish: Brain barrier selective gate maari irukkum. Placenta-la many drugs baby-ku pogalam.

Real-life example: Levodopa crosses BBB; dopamine does not cross well.

Memory trick: BBB = brain bouncer.

Redistribution

Simple English: Highly lipid-soluble drugs may first enter brain, then move to muscle and fat, ending effect before elimination is complete.

Tanglish: Thiopental brain-ku fast pogum, apram fat/muscle-ku redistribute aagum; effect short.

Real-life example: Thiopental anesthesia wears off due to redistribution.

Memory trick: Redistribution = drug changes seats.

Formula Explanations and Solved Problems

Volume of distribution

Vd = Amount of drug in body / Plasma concentration

Why used: Estimate tissue distribution and loading dose.

  • Vd: apparent volume
  • Amount: drug in body
  • Plasma concentration: measured blood level

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Dose 500 mg gives Cp 10 mg/L. Vd=500/10=50 L.

Loading dose

LD = Target Cp x Vd / F

Why used: Reach therapeutic level quickly.

  • LD: loading dose
  • Target Cp: desired plasma concentration
  • F: bioavailability

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Target 5 mg/L, Vd 40 L, F 0.5. LD=5x40/0.5=400 mg.

Graph Explanations

Two-phase distribution curve

X-axis time; Y-axis plasma concentration. Initial steep fall shows distribution; later slower fall shows elimination.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks to identify distribution phase and elimination phase.

Binding curve

X-axis drug concentration; Y-axis fraction bound. Saturable binding plateaus when protein sites fill.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks why free drug rises sharply after saturation.
