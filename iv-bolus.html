Intravenous Bolus Injection

Rapid injection of an IV dose directly into systemic circulation.

Pharmacokinetics, One-compartment modelEnglish + TanglishExam focused

First Understand

What is topic? Rapid injection of an IV dose directly into systemic circulation.

Why important? IV bolus gives immediate 100 percent bioavailability and is the simplest model for estimating Vd, clearance, half-life, and elimination rate constant.

Clinical applications: Emergency drug administration, loading doses, antimicrobial PK, therapeutic drug monitoring, and interpreting semilog concentration-time plots.

Every Heading and Subheading

One-Compartment Assumption

Simple English: After mixing, the body behaves like one uniform compartment and elimination follows first-order kinetics.

Tanglish: Drug body-la uniform-aa mix aagudhu nu assume pannuvom.

Real-life example: Aminoglycoside levels are often interpreted with compartment models.

Memory trick: One box model.

Initial Concentration C0

Simple English: C0 is concentration immediately after IV bolus and complete mixing; it is estimated by extrapolating the elimination line to time zero.

Tanglish: Injection apram zero time-la expected concentration dhaan C0.

Real-life example: Used to calculate Vd.

Memory trick: C0 is start point.

Elimination Rate Constant

Simple English: k is fraction of drug removed per unit time in first-order elimination.

Tanglish: Oru time-la fraction evlo remove aagudho k sollum.

Real-life example: Higher k means shorter half-life.

Memory trick: k kicks drug out.

Semilog Plot

Simple English: Log concentration vs time becomes a straight line for first-order elimination.

Tanglish: Log graph-la straight line vandha first-order elimination.

Real-life example: Slope gives k.

Memory trick: Straight semilog = first order.

Formula Explanations and Solved Problems

Concentration-time

C = C0 x e^(-k t)

Why used: Predict plasma level after IV bolus.

  • C: concentration at time t
  • C0: initial concentration
  • k: elimination rate constant
  • t: time

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: C0 20 mg/L, k 0.2 h-1, t 5 h. C=20xe^-1=7.36 mg/L.

Half-life

t1/2 = 0.693 / k

Why used: Convert between k and half-life.

  • t1/2: half-life
  • k: elimination rate constant

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: k 0.231 h-1. t1/2=0.693/0.231=3 h.

Vd after IV bolus

Vd = Dose / C0

Why used: Estimate distribution volume.

  • Dose: IV dose
  • C0: initial concentration

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Dose 1000 mg, C0 20 mg/L. Vd=50 L.

Clearance

CL = k x Vd

Why used: Find clearance from bolus data.

  • CL: total clearance
  • k: elimination rate constant
  • Vd: volume of distribution

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: k 0.1 h-1, Vd 40 L. CL=4 L/h.

Graph Explanations

IV bolus concentration curve

X-axis time; Y-axis concentration. Curve starts at C0 and declines exponentially.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks to calculate k, t1/2, Vd, CL from graph.

Semilog straight line

X-axis time; Y-axis log concentration. Slope equals -k/2.303.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks to derive k from two concentrations.
