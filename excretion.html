Excretion

Removal of unchanged drug and metabolites from the body, mainly by kidney and bile.

Pharmacokinetics, ADMEEnglish + TanglishExam focused

First Understand

What is topic? Removal of unchanged drug and metabolites from the body, mainly by kidney and bile.

Why important? Excretion determines clearance, half-life, dose interval, accumulation, and toxicity risk in renal disease.

Clinical applications: Renal dose adjustment, dialysis decisions, urine pH manipulation in poisoning, monitoring aminoglycosides and lithium.

Every Heading and Subheading

Renal Filtration

Simple English: Free unbound drug filters through glomerulus into urine.

Tanglish: Free drug kidney filter-la urine-ku pogum.

Real-life example: Highly protein-bound drugs filter less.

Memory trick: Only free can filter.

Tubular Secretion

Simple English: Transporters actively move drugs from blood into tubular fluid.

Tanglish: Kidney tubule active transporter use panni drug-ai urine side-ku push pannum.

Real-life example: Penicillin is secreted; probenecid blocks secretion.

Memory trick: Secretion pushes drug out.

Tubular Reabsorption

Simple English: Unionized lipid-soluble drug can diffuse back from urine to blood.

Tanglish: Unionized drug urine-lendhu back blood-ku pogalam.

Real-life example: Weak acid poisoning can be treated by alkalinizing urine.

Memory trick: Ionized stays inside urine.

Biliary Excretion

Simple English: Liver secretes some drugs/metabolites into bile and intestine.

Tanglish: Liver bile moolama drug intestine-ku send pannum.

Real-life example: Some glucuronides undergo enterohepatic cycling.

Memory trick: Bile can recycle.

Clearance and Half-Life

Simple English: Clearance is volume of plasma cleared per time; half-life is time for concentration to fall by 50 percent.

Tanglish: Clearance-na blood clean aagura speed. Half-life-na concentration half aagura time.

Real-life example: Renal impairment reduces clearance and increases half-life.

Memory trick: Clearance cleans; half-life halves.

Formula Explanations and Solved Problems

Renal clearance

CLr = Ae / AUC

Why used: Measure kidney removal of unchanged drug.

  • CLr: renal clearance
  • Ae: amount excreted unchanged in urine
  • AUC: plasma exposure

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Ae 300 mg, AUC 50 mg.h/L. CLr=6 L/h.

Fraction excreted unchanged

fe = Ae / Dose

Why used: Know renal contribution to elimination.

  • fe: fraction excreted unchanged
  • Ae: unchanged amount in urine

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Ae 40 mg from 100 mg dose. fe=0.4 or 40%.

Half-life

t1/2 = 0.693 x Vd / CL

Why used: Predict accumulation and washout.

  • t1/2: half-life
  • Vd: volume of distribution
  • CL: total clearance

How to remember: identify output first, then place exposure/concentration/time terms around it.

Step example: Vd 50 L, CL 5 L/h. t1/2=0.693x50/5=6.93 h.

Graph Explanations

Urinary excretion rate plot

X-axis time; Y-axis excretion rate. Decline parallels plasma concentration in first-order elimination.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks how to estimate elimination rate constant from slope.

Cumulative amount excreted plot

X-axis time; Y-axis cumulative amount excreted. It approaches a plateau when excretion is complete.

X-axis: Time or concentration depending on graph. Y-axis: concentration, rate, percent dissolved, or cumulative amount.

How exams ask: Exam asks to identify total amount excreted unchanged.
